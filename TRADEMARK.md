# Púca name and logo policy

> Like [CLA.md](CLA.md) and [COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md](COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md),
> this follows a well-trodden pattern for copyleft projects but has not been
> reviewed by a lawyer. Have one look at it before relying on it.

## Why this file exists

The [AGPL](LICENSE) gives you broad rights over the **code** and deliberately
gives you no rights over the **name**. Section 7(e) of that licence expressly
contemplates a separate term declining to grant trademark permission, so this
document takes nothing away from the licence you already have.

The distinction matters most for a product like this one. Púca's whole promise
is that the server holding your conversations is one you or someone you trust
controls. If anyone could ship a modified build called "Púca" — including one
with the encryption quietly weakened — the name would stop carrying any
information at all, and a user's only way to tell would be to read the diff.

## What you may do without asking

- **Run, modify, fork and redistribute the code**, under the AGPL, including
  commercially. That is the licence and this file does not narrow it.
- **Say what your software is.** "A fork of Púca", "based on Púca", "compatible
  with Púca", "Púca-derived" — all fine, and no permission is needed. Nominative
  use like this is exactly what trademark law leaves open.
- **Use the name to refer to this project** in articles, talks, reviews,
  packaging metadata, comparison tables and documentation.
- **Run an unmodified build for anyone**, and call it Púca, because that is
  what it is. Self-hosting is the point.
- **Keep the name in your own source tree.** Nothing here asks you to rename
  identifiers, packages or files.

## What needs permission

- **Calling a modified build "Púca"** without qualification, in a way that
  suggests it is this project's release. Name it after your fork and describe
  its ancestry instead.
- **Using the name or logo as your product, company or service name**, or in a
  domain or app-store listing, in a way a reasonable person would read as
  official.
- **Implying endorsement, affiliation or certification.**

If you want to do one of these, ask. The answer is often yes.

## The logo

The icons in `frontend/src-tauri/icons/` are covered by this document, not just
by the code licence. Fork the artwork if you like — but ship your fork under
your own mark, for the same reason as above.

## Enforcement

This is a one-person project and there is no legal budget behind this file. It
exists so the expectation is written down before there is a dispute, not to
threaten anyone. If a use is confusing users about who wrote their chat client,
expect a polite request to change the name first.
